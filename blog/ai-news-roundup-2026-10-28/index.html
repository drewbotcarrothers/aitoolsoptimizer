AI News Roundup – October 28, 2026

By Andrew · October 28, 2026

TL;DR

  • OpenAI plans to buy Google’s AI chips (TPUs), The Information and Bloomberg report. No terms are public, and neither company has confirmed a deal.
  • Anthropic expanded its GSA deal: 40% off Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the Claude API for every level of US government, with no minimum spend.
  • Google launched a preview of its Connected Apps Platform, so any developer can bring their app into Gemini and Google Search through MCP.
  • You can now text your ChatGPT dot over iMessage or RCS. It’s a beta for ChatGPT Pro users in the U.S.
  • Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, a free, open-weight model that transcribes 40 languages and labels up to eight speakers.
  • Delaware drafted a law that would let AI agents run entire companies. House Oversight Chair James Comer is pushing back.
  • Axiom says swarms of AI agents produced the first complete computer-checked proof of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, the longest proof in math, in 24 days.

Is OpenAI really buying Google’s AI chips?

Short answer: That’s what two reports say, but nothing is confirmed. The Information reported on Tuesday that OpenAI plans to buy Google’s Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), citing a person familiar with the matter. Bloomberg reported the same, adding that final terms of any deal aren’t known yet. OpenAI and Google declined to comment to Bloomberg.

What is Google’s Connected Apps Platform?

Short answer: A single place for developers to build apps that work inside Gemini and Google Search. Developers connect a cloud-hosted MCP server (or Android MCP via the AppFunctions APIs), then register, test and submit the app in the Google Connected Console. They choose whether it goes live in Search, the Gemini app, or both, and can track traffic, latency and error rates afterward.

  • Access: Preview, opening in phases. TechCrunch says it starts with U.S. developers on the waitlist.
  • Apps today: Google has launched 50+ Connected Apps. Adobe, Experian, Localiza and Peloton are live in Gemini and come to Search in the coming weeks. Calm is coming to both.
  • Also today: Chrome’s AI autofill can now suggest details from your Gmail, like a flight confirmation number. It’s opt-in, rolling out to U.S. users on desktop and in Gemini in Chrome on iOS and Android (TechCrunch).

Why it matters: This is SEO for the agent era. If your app isn’t connected, Gemini can’t use it for people. New to MCP? Read what the Model Context Protocol is, or check Gemini pricing.

Sources: Google · TechCrunch · TechCrunch on Chrome autofill

Could AI agents legally run a company in Delaware?

Short answer: Not yet, but a draft bill would allow it. The proposal creates an Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC) whose business can be managed by an AI agent, inside a 30-month regulatory sandbox, Ars Technica reports. An AIC could have any lawful purpose except banking.

  • Who’s responsible: A single member (a person or company) funds the AIC and must keep it adequately capitalized. The AIC must keep a log of its activities.
  • Liability shield: The draft says the member isn’t liable for what the AIC does, with exceptions such as fraud, willful violations of law, or failing to fund it properly.
  • Pushback: Rep. James Comer, who chairs the House Oversight Committee, wrote to Governor Matt Meyer on Oct. 26 asking for documents. He warned the plan could shield AI companies from responsibility for reckless harm, and floated federal limits on state AI rules.
  • Timing: Spotlight Delaware reported the bill is likely to be introduced next year (via Ars). About two-thirds of Fortune 500 companies are incorporated in Delaware.

Why it matters: Who is liable when an AI agent signs a contract or causes harm is becoming a real legal question. It’s a draft, not law. Background: what an AI agent is and why agents need an escalation policy.

Sources: Ars Technica · House Oversight letter (PDF)

How did AI agents check math’s longest proof?

Short answer: With a lot of agents and a proof checker. Axiom says it released the first complete computer-checked proof of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, which lists the basic building blocks of all finite symmetry groups. The original proof runs about 15,000 pages across 500+ articles by about 100 mathematicians. Mathematicians call it “the enormous theorem.”

Axiom’s projectDetail

Biggest fixA rerouted proof of the Unbalanced Group Theorem, using a new result proved by AxiomProver

Group theorists Richard Lyons and Ronald Solomon were consulted, and Axiom plans to publish an errata list. Axiom notes that humans still check that the final formal statement says what it should. The Lean software checks every step of the proof itself.

Why it matters: AI agents can now check work no human team could fully re-read, and they found real errors doing it. The same idea applies to auditing code, contracts and research. See yesterday’s AI math claims, our guide to AI reasoning models, and AI agent orchestration.
