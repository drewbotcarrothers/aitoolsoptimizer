How to Create a Feature Flag Rollout Plan with AI (2026): The Slice, the Clock, the Owner

By Andrew · October 24, 2026

A feature-flag rollout plan written with AI is a one-page record of the flag name, the cohort, the watch numbers, the clock, and the person who can turn the flag off. The model may format notes you paste. It may not invent a percentage, a success metric, or a rollback step. The switch itself is what AI feature flags are. Who sees the new behavior first is what an AI canary release is. The version you place on the path is what an AI canary deployment is.

The page you open when the watch numbers go bad is what an AI runbook is. Moving back to the fixed version later is what AI rollforward is.

What fits on the plan

BlockWho owns itThe model may
FlagThe name that exists in the systemCopy it. It may not rename it to sound clearer

A plan with no rollback owner is a launch note. Do not start the clock. A plan that changes the prompt, the model, and the tool list under one flag is three plans. Split them so the off switch means one thing.

A prompt that holds the line:

“Format a feature-flag rollout plan from the flag name, cohort, version id, watch counts, clock, and rollback owner I pasted. Do not add a percentage or a metric. If rollback is missing, write Rollback missing and stop. Do not tell me to turn the flag on.”

What not to delegate

Do not ask the model whether it is safe to go to 100 percent. Do not let the plan and the flag config drift. The name on the page has to be the name in the system. Do not roll out a write you cannot undo and call the flag a safety control. The off switch stops new traffic. It does not unsend the email the new version already sent. That case belongs in the timeline workflow: how to write an incident timeline with AI.

FAQ

How is this different from the canary glossary pages?

Those pages define the slice and the version on the path. This page is the document a person signs before the slice starts: names, numbers, clock, owner. You can understand a canary and still launch without a plan. The plan is the missing artifact.

Can the model suggest watch metrics?

It can repeat metrics you already collect. It cannot invent an error budget. If you have no band for a count, the block says Band missing. A rollout without a band is a demo.

What if the flag is for a prompt, not code?

The same blocks. The version id is the prompt version. The path is whatever serves that prompt. Prompt versioning is what prompt versioning is. The plan does not get to skip the rollback owner because the change is “only words.”

Who is allowed to widen the slice?

The owner on the plan, after the clock, if the watch counts stayed inside the band. A channel poll is not a widening. Write the new cohort on the plan before you change the flag.
