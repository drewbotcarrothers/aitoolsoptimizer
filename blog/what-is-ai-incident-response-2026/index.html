What Is AI Incident Response? (2026): The Page, the Flag, the Note

By Andrew · October 24, 2026

AI incident response is the written path a team follows when a model-backed feature is wrong, down, or spending in a way you did not approve: a severity, an owner, a way to turn the feature off, a customer note, and a short record afterward. This page is that path as operations and documentation. It does not describe attack techniques, payloads, or how to probe a system.

The file the on-call opens is what an AI runbook is. The switch that turns a model off without a redeploy is what AI feature flags are. The promise you can check is what AI SLA monitoring is. Traces and cost you can open are what AI observability is.

Untrusted text in a document is a product risk to write down in the runbook, not a technique to practice here. The boundary, at the level of roles and checks, is what a prompt injection defense is. The person who approves the customer sentence is what human-in-the-loop AI is.

What to leave out of the channel

Leave exploit steps, credentials, and customer payloads out of the status note and out of the prompt. Say what customers can do now. Say when you will update. A guessed root cause is a hallucination with a timestamp.

If spend is the incident, the ceiling you wish you had set is what an AI budget cap is. The spike itself is what AI cost anomaly detection is.

FAQ

Is a chatbot outage an AI incident?

Treat it as one when the failure is the model, the tool it called, the retrieval it used, or the bill it ran up. A vendor status page can still be the cause. Your note should match what you know, including “we are waiting on the vendor.”

Who is allowed to turn the feature off?

The people named in the runbook. A well-written flag is useless if the on-call does not have access. Write the access next to the flag name.

Can AI write the postmortem?

It can turn a timeline you paste into a draft. It cannot attend the incident. Every time, name, and customer impact in the draft has to match the log. The check is how to fact-check AI writing.

Do we need a separate process from the rest of production?

Use the same severity names and the same status page if you already have them. Add the model-specific lines: which flag, which key, which prompt version, and who approves turning it back on.
