AI News Roundup – November 13, 2026

By Andrew · November 13, 2026

TL;DR

  • OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, built with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. Your ChatGPT dot (agent) can now pay for things for you, but it never sees your real card number, and you approve every purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint. It starts with ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US.
  • OpenAI added Daybreak Red Team (agents that pen-test web apps) and Daybreak Defense Factory (agents that draft security patches for engineers to review).
  • Google and Samsung opened preorders for Gemini-powered Intelligent Eyewear from Warby Parker (from $395) and Gentle Monster ($449–$489), shipping December 10.
  • Anthropic opened a private beta of self-hosted Claude Managed Agents: the whole agent service runs on your own infrastructure.
  • SpaceX plans more than $74 billion for two AI supercomputing campuses in Arizona and Texas.
  • OpenAI disbanded its stand-alone robotics team, betting its general models can run robots (Fortune).

Today’s take: ChatGPT just went from finding the sweater to buying it. The smart part is the design: your agent gets a one-off payment token, not your card, and nothing is bought without your fingerprint or face. The catch is the price of entry: it’s Pro-only, and ChatGPT Pro costs $100, $200 or $500 a month (see our ChatGPT pricing guide). If you’re on Plus, wait; this will tell us whether agent shopping actually works before it reaches cheaper plans.

What is ChatGPT Wallet, and how does it keep your card safe?

Short answer: A payment feature for OpenAI’s always-on dots agents, announced November 13. You save a card once, ask your dot to shop or book something, and approve the final purchase yourself.

StepWhat happens (per OpenAI)
1. Add a cardOnce, by asking your dot to add a card to Wallet
2. Your dot shopsIt finds the item or booking you asked for
3. You reviewMerchant, items, total and selected card
4. You approveSecurity check such as Face ID or a fingerprint, for every purchase
5. PaymentThe card network issues a payment token (substitute card number, security code and expiry). The agent and the business never see your real card

You keep your card’s rewards and its protections for eligible disputes (for example, a defective item). OpenAI says some websites may not support agent purchases yet, and that approval alone doesn’t confirm an order, so check the merchant’s confirmation.

Who can use it now:

RequirementDetail
PlanChatGPT Pro (Pro is $100, $200 or $500 a month, per our ChatGPT page)
Age18 or older
CountryUS, with an eligible US-issued card and US billing address
WhereInside dots, rolling out gradually

OpenAI says that later you’ll be able to pre-approve purchases within spending limits, categories or time periods. For businesses, it published a Commerce Partner Playbook (product feeds, letting signed agent traffic through with Web Bot Auth, plugins and WebMCP) and is working with fraud and payment firms including Forter, Riskified, Sardine, Signifyd, Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe. The Decoder notes Wallet replaces OpenAI’s scrapped Instant Checkout.

Why it matters to you: It’s the first time ChatGPT’s agent can finish a purchase rather than hand you a link, with a safety design (tokens plus a biometric check on every buy) that’s easy to understand. If you already pay for Pro and dots, try it on one small, low-risk purchase first. Everyone else: compare plans in our ChatGPT review and read what an AI agent is and AI agent budget caps.

Sources: OpenAI: Introducing ChatGPT Wallet · ChatGPT release notes · CNBC · 9to5Mac · The Decoder

What do OpenAI’s Daybreak Red Team and Defense Factory do?

Short answer: Two security products announced at OpenAI’s Cyber at the Frontier event on November 12.

ProductWhat it doesAccess
Daybreak Red TeamA team of agents pen-tests your web apps using source code and app context, validates findings from scanners, bug bounties and disclosure reports, and checks whether a patch stops the exploit. Runs in isolated environments with Guardian review limiting what agents can reachEarly access, by request
Daybreak Defense FactoryAgents trace vulnerabilities through your code and draft patches; imports findings from Wiz, Snyk and Endor Labs; scans GitHub repos and new commits; works with Codex and opens draft pull requests. Engineers review and test before merge; nothing deploys automaticallyPreview plugin in ChatGPT for workspaces with Daybreak access

Why it matters to you: Finding bugs was never the hard part for busy teams; fixing them was. If your workspace has Daybreak access, point Defense Factory at one repo you know well and count how many patches you’d merge as-is. See Codex pricing explained and AI tools for security engineers.

Sources: OpenAI: Daybreak Red Team · OpenAI: Daybreak Defense Factory · SecurityWeek · Neowin

Can you now run Claude Managed Agents on your own servers?

Short answer: Yes, in private beta (November 12). Self-hosted Claude Managed Agents puts the whole Managed Agents service, not just the sandboxes, in your own cloud or data center. You store the agents, credentials, sessions and session history, and you choose where model requests go: Anthropic by default, or Amazon Bedrock, Google’s Agent Platform, Microsoft Foundry or your own gateway. Zero data retention applies as it does to other Messages API calls. Access is through your Anthropic account team; no public price yet.

Why it matters to you: Banks, hospitals and other regulated teams that couldn’t let agent data leave their walls now have a way to run Claude agents. See Claude pricing explained.

Sources: Claude Platform release notes · Anthropic

Sources: SpaceX · Quartz · The Hill

Why did OpenAI disband its robotics team?

Short answer: Fortune reported on November 13 that OpenAI broke up its stand-alone robotics team. Robotics researchers moved to the main research team under Chief Research Officer Mark Chen, and applied robotics and hardware moved to the Applied organization under President Greg Brockman. OpenAI told Fortune robotics remains core and the change is meant to speed it up. Fortune says researchers saw surprising results connecting GPT-6 Astra directly to robots, and that Sam Altman is targeting a first public demo of OpenAI’s humanoid robot in 2027.

Why it matters to you: The bet is that the same model behind ChatGPT can also drive robots, so improvements in the chatbot you use flow straight into the physical world.

Sources: Fortune
