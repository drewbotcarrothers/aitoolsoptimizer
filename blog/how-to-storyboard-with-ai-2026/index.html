How to Storyboard with AI (2026): Frames First, Motion Second

By Andrew · October 24, 2026

A storyboard is a sequence of shots you can point at, not a pile of pretty frames. Write the shots in words first. Generate a frame only for a shot you are willing to film or animate. Spend motion credits on the frames you kept. This page does not score which generator looks more cinematic.

Step 1: Write the board before you generate

Number the shots. For each one, write who is in frame, what changes, and what the viewer should understand. Six shots are a board. Forty adjectives are a mood, and a mood is how you burn a credit balance on frames you cannot sequence.

Napkin AI can turn that list into a diagram you can drop in a deck. Free covers light use at 500 credits a week. Plus at $9 a person a month adds PPT and SVG export. The review is Napkin AI review. A diagram is not a frame. It is the order of the frames.

Paste the same list into Gamma if the client needs to see the board as slides. Plus is $9 a seat. The credit rules are Gamma pricing explained.

Step 2: Generate the frame that matches the shot

If the shot has words in the picture, a poster, a phone UI, a title card, start with Ideogram. Plus at $15 a month on yearly billing includes private generation. Free images are not private. The review is Ideogram review.

If the shot is a look, and the words will be typeset later in Canva or a design tool, Midjourney Standard at $30 a month adds unlimited Relax image generation. Basic at $10 does not. There is no free plan. The GPU bill is Midjourney pricing explained. Companies over $1 million in gross revenue need Pro or Mega, at $60 or $120 a month, for the commercial rule on that page.

If you may train a style or you want video inside the same account, Leonardo Premium at $30 a month adds unlimited relaxed images for select models. Fast tokens still spend on full-speed work. The review is Leonardo.Ai review. The three-way with FLUX is Midjourney vs Leonardo vs FLUX.

Generate two frames per shot, not twenty. Pick one. A storyboard that keeps every variation is a folder, not a plan.

Step 3: Move one frame, and price it

Google Flow is the motion test that has a published credit cost in this directory. Everyone gets 50 credits a day, and unused daily credits do not roll over. Veo 3.1 Lite is 10 credits a clip, Fast is 20, and Quality is 100. A Quality clip is two days of the free daily grant. Google AI Plus at $4.99 a month adds 200 monthly Flow credits. Pro at $19.99 adds 1,000. The review is Google Flow review.

Test the one shot where motion changes the idea: a door opening, a product turn, a face turning. A static frame does not need a clip. Runway Standard is $12 a month billed annually for 625 credits if the motion work moves out of Flow. Do not run the same shot in three video products “to compare” until you have a frame you would actually use. That habit is how storyboards become invoices.

Kling is the other video quote. Its renewal prices are easy to misread because the directory records first-month discounts and later rates. Read Runway vs Kling vs Veo and the live card before you subscribe for a board.

FAQ

What AI tool makes a storyboard?

None of them makes the sequence for you. You write the shots. Ideogram, Leonardo, or Midjourney makes a frame. Napkin or Gamma shows the order. Flow or Runway moves a frame you kept.

Is Midjourney free for storyboards?

No. Basic is $10 a month, or $8 billed annually. Ideogram and Leonardo have free tiers you can use to test a single frame.

How do I keep text readable in a frame?

Use Ideogram for the frame that contains words. Put other type in Canva or your design tool afterward. Midjourney is the weaker choice when the letters are the point.

Can I storyboard a whole ad in Google Flow?

You can, and Quality clips at 100 credits each will spend a free daily grant quickly. Board the ad as stills first. Move the shots that need motion.
