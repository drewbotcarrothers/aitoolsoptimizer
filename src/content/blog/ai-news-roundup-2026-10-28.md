## Is OpenAI really buying Google’s AI chips?

**Short answer:** That’s what two reports say, but nothing is confirmed. **The Information** reported on Tuesday that OpenAI plans to buy Google’s **Tensor Processing Units (TPUs)**, citing a person familiar with the matter. **Bloomberg** reported the same, adding that final terms of any deal aren’t known yet. OpenAI and Google declined to comment to Bloomberg.

| What we know | Source |

| OpenAI also plans to start using **its own chips**, built with Broadcom, later this year | Bloomberg |

Reuters notes that Google began selling TPUs directly to some customers this year, and that deals with **Anthropic** have fueled TPU demand. Last week, OpenAI said its frontier models are coming to Google Cloud. Alphabet reports quarterly results later today.

**Why it matters:** More chip suppliers means more capacity for the AI you use every day, and less dependence on one vendor. It also shows how rivals like OpenAI and Google now buy from each other. ## What is Google’s Connected Apps Platform?

**Short answer:** A single place for developers to build apps that work **inside Gemini and Google Search**. Developers connect a cloud-hosted **MCP server** (or Android MCP via the AppFunctions APIs), then register, test and submit the app in the **Google Connected Console**. They choose whether it goes live in Search, the Gemini app, or both, and can track traffic, latency and error rates afterward.

- **Access:** Preview, opening in phases. TechCrunch says it starts with **U.S. developers** on the waitlist.
- **Apps today:** Google has launched **50+ Connected Apps**. **Adobe**, **Experian**, **Localiza** and **Peloton** are live in Gemini and come to Search in the coming weeks. **Calm** is coming to both.
- **Also today:** Chrome’s AI autofill can now suggest details from your **Gmail**, like a flight confirmation number. It’s opt-in, rolling out to U.S. users on desktop and in Gemini in Chrome on iOS and Android (TechCrunch).

**Why it matters:** This is SEO for the agent era. If your app isn’t connected, Gemini can’t use it for people. New to MCP? Read [what the Model Context Protocol is](/blog/what-is-model-context-protocol-2026/), or check [Gemini pricing](/blog/gemini-pricing-explained-2026/).

## Can you text your ChatGPT dot now?

**Short answer:** Yes, if you’re a **ChatGPT Pro** user in the **U.S.** OpenAI’s always-on agents, called **dots**, can now be reached by **iMessage**, or **RCS** on Android. It’s a beta, and one-to-one only.

| Texting your dot | Detail (OpenAI) |
|---|---|
| Who | ChatGPT Pro users in the U.S. (beta) |
| Send | Text, photos and voice messages |
| Replies | Your dot can text you proactively with updates |
| Approvals | It sends a link to review the request in ChatGPT |
| Setup | Dot profile → **Texting** → verify your number → save the contact card |
| Not supported | Group chats, phone calls, FaceTime |

OpenAI says your dot keeps the same context and plugins wherever you reach it, and texting doesn’t give the dot or OpenAI access to your other text conversations.

**Why it matters:** Always-on agents are moving into the apps you already check all day. You can hand off a task from your messages without opening ChatGPT. Learn more in our [OpenAI dots guide](/tools/openai-dots/) and [dots pricing explained](/blog/openai-dots-pricing-explained-2026/).

## What is Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe?

**Short answer:** A **free, open-weight** speech model that does transcription, speaker labels, translation and audio Q&A in one checkpoint. You can guide it with plain-language prompts, like “Transcribe this recording and label each speaker.”

| Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe | Nvidia’s figure |
|---|---|
| Languages transcribed | **40** |
| Translation to/from English | **31** languages |
| Speaker labels | Up to **8** speakers, in **13** languages |
| Max recording | About **1 hour** |
| Size | **30B** parameters, about **4B** active per token |
| Open ASR Leaderboard (English) | **#1** open-weight model, **5.33%** average WER |

It works on **recorded audio, not live streams**. Nvidia also released **Granary v2**, an open speech dataset covering **463,062 hours** in **48 languages**. The model is on Hugging Face and ModelScope, with quantized versions for local use “coming soon.” Leaderboard claims are Nvidia’s.

**Why it matters:** Free, high-quality transcription you can run yourself is good news for meeting notes, podcasts and medical dictation, especially if your audio can’t leave your own servers. ## Could AI agents legally run a company in Delaware?

**Short answer:** Not yet, but a draft bill would allow it. The proposal creates an **Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC)** whose business can be managed by an **AI agent**, inside a **30-month regulatory sandbox**, Ars Technica reports. An AIC could have any lawful purpose **except banking**.

- **Who’s responsible:** A single member (a person or company) funds the AIC and must keep it adequately capitalized. The AIC must keep a log of its activities.
- **Liability shield:** The draft says the member isn’t liable for what the AIC does, with exceptions such as fraud, willful violations of law, or failing to fund it properly.
- **Pushback:** Rep. **James Comer**, who chairs the House Oversight Committee, wrote to Governor **Matt Meyer** on Oct. 26 asking for documents. He warned the plan could shield AI companies from responsibility for reckless harm, and floated federal limits on state AI rules.
- **Timing:** Spotlight Delaware reported the bill is likely to be introduced next year (via Ars). About two-thirds of Fortune 500 companies are incorporated in Delaware.

**Why it matters:** Who is liable when an AI agent signs a contract or causes harm is becoming a real legal question. It’s a draft, not law. ## How did AI agents check math’s longest proof?

**Short answer:** With a lot of agents and a proof checker. **Axiom** says it released the first complete **computer-checked proof** of the **Classification of Finite Simple Groups**, which lists the basic building blocks of all finite symmetry groups. The original proof runs about **15,000 pages** across **500+ articles** by about **100 mathematicians**. Mathematicians call it “the enormous theorem.”

| Axiom’s project | Detail |
|---|---|
| Time | **24 days** |
| Workers | Engineers and mathematicians directing swarms of **dozens of AI agents** |
| Output | About **43.1 million lines** of Lean code |
| Problems found | **34** noteworthy deviations from the published record |
| Biggest fix | A rerouted proof of the Unbalanced Group Theorem, using a **new result** proved by AxiomProver |

Group theorists Richard Lyons and Ronald Solomon were consulted, and Axiom plans to publish an errata list. Axiom notes that humans still check that the final formal statement says what it should. The Lean software checks every step of the proof itself.

**Why it matters:** AI agents can now check work no human team could fully re-read, and they found real errors doing it. The same idea applies to auditing code, contracts and research.
