## Why are Elvis Presley’s music owners suing Anthropic?

**Short answer:** They say Anthropic copied Presley’s songs to train Claude without permission. The lawsuit was filed on **Friday, October 23**, by a unit of **Authentic Brands Group**, which largely controls Elvis’s intellectual property, including much of his song catalog, and was co-filed by other entities that own Elvis rights, Billboard reports.

| Detail | What the lawsuit says |
|---|---|
| Songs at issue | **274** Presley compositions |
| Examples | “Can’t Help Falling in Love,” “All Shook Up,” “Don’t Be Cruel,” “A Little Less Conversation” |
| Damages sought | Up to **$150,000 per song**, about **$41 million** (The Guardian) |
| Key claim | Anthropic’s guardrails on song outputs were ineffective |

Lead attorney Richard Busch wrote that Anthropic “has built its entire business model based on copyright infringement at a scale never seen before.” The Guardian calls it one of the first major legal challenges brought directly by an artist’s estate against an AI developer. AI companies argue that training is protected by fair use, a question still pending across dozens of lawsuits, Billboard notes. A rep for Anthropic did not immediately respond to Billboard’s request for comment.

**Why it matters:** The fair-use fight over AI training is far from settled, and music is now one of its main battlegrounds. If you write lyrics or songs with AI tools like [Claude](/tools/claude/), [Suno](/tools/suno/) or [Udio](/tools/udio/), don’t copy or closely imitate real songs.

## What is the UK’s new AI Biosecurity Lab?

**Short answer:** A new independent, non-profit organization that will use frontier AI to spot biological threats earlier and speed up vaccines and treatments. The UK government calls it the **world’s first dedicated AI biosecurity organisation**. It was announced on **October 24**.

| Backer | Commitment (per GOV.UK) |
|---|---|
| UK government | **£100 million** in funding (about **$134 million**, per Bloomberg) |
| OpenAI | **£100 million** in compute and model credits, plus trusted access to its most capable relevant models through its Rosalind Biodefense programme |
| Anthropic | Up to **£100 million** in model credits, trusted model access and technical support |
| Google DeepMind | Research collaborations; will look to provide trusted access to frontier Gemini and specialised science models |
| Basecamp Research | Access to its AI-biology models and expertise |

GOV.UK says it will work much like the UK’s AI Security Institute, with the autonomy to move like a frontier AI–bio company. According to City AM and the BBC, it’s part of a wider package announced ahead of the UK Budget that also gives the AI Security Institute an extra **£120 million**.

**Why it matters:** Frontier labs are now paying directly for government safety work. The same models you use for research, like [ChatGPT](/tools/chatgpt/), [Claude](/tools/claude/) and [Gemini](/tools/gemini/), are being pointed at pandemic defense.

**Sources:** [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/introducing-the-ai-biosecurity-lab) · [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/uk-vows-100-million-for-biosecurity-lab-with-openai-anthropic) · [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/google-openai-and-anthropic-back-100m-uk-ai-biosecurity-lab/) · [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqdjv8xllvyyo)

## Why is Texas suing OpenAI?

**Short answer:** Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton says OpenAI marketed ChatGPT as safe and reliable but failed to protect Texans, including children. He sued **OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman** on **October 23** under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices–Consumer Protection Act.

According to the Texas Tribune, the case was filed in Collin County district court. It asks for a wide-ranging injunction that would require OpenAI to **block minors** from the product and get **third-party approval of new models** from a court-appointed evaluator, among other restrictions. The Tribune also notes that Paxton is the GOP nominee for U.S. Senate and that Elon Musk is the largest single donor behind his campaign.

An OpenAI spokesperson told the Tribune the company has worked with experts to mitigate risks and is “committed to working with Texas and other states on advancing pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry — not just one company.”

## Is AI paying off for companies outside Big Tech?

**Short answer:** Not yet, according to Apollo chief economist **Torsten Slok**. In an October 24 note he wrote: “Despite massive investments and widespread hype around artificial intelligence, the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech.”

| Measure (Apollo note) | “Mag 7” tech giants | “S&P 493” (everyone else) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue per worker | Rising | Flat |
| Profit margins | Rising | Flat |
| Productivity growth from AI | No signs yet across the economy | No signs yet across the economy |

The “S&P 493” is the S&P 500 minus Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla. Slok says mainstream companies are starting to see token spending “not as a source of growth but as a cost center,” and adds: “AI may be a great calculator, but in business, intelligence is mostly judgment.” TechCrunch reported the note on October 25.

## Why is Sweden’s government blocking ChatGPT and Claude?

**Short answer:** Information security. From **Monday, October 26**, the browser versions of **ChatGPT** and **Claude** can no longer be reached from staff computers at Sweden’s Government Offices (Regeringskansliet), Sveriges Radio reports. Staff are directed to approved tools, including **Microsoft Copilot**, which the Government Offices reviewed and then made more secure.

Digitalisation director Mikael Wettercrantz wrote on the staff intranet that using AI services without agreements can have consequences for information security. The block isn’t total: the Government Offices told SVT that staff can still reach the ChatGPT and Claude language models through **Intric**, its internal AI platform. Sweden’s Tax Agency and the National Board of Institutional Care already have similar blocks, and the Enforcement Authority is preparing a decision.

## What is Microduck, and why did 20,000 people preorder one?

**Short answer:** It’s a small open-source walking robot from **Pollen Robotics**, part of **Hugging Face**. After a viral video of Nvidia CEO **Jensen Huang** playing with Microducks during his GTC Berlin keynote, preorders passed **20,000 units** by the next day, Hugging Face’s Clem Delangue said, TechCrunch reports.

| Spec (Pollen Robotics) | Microduck |
|---|---|
| Price | **$399** before taxes and shipping |
| Height | **25 cm** biped |
| Motors | **15** |
| Sensors | Camera, plus LiDAR and two IMUs |
| Out of the box | **7** trained moves, including walk, sit and stand, kick, grab, roller skating and getting back up |
| Software | SDK, simulation and RL training stack on GitHub (Apache-2.0) |

Owners can train new tricks in simulation with reinforcement learning, deploy them to the robot and share them with the community. TechCrunch says Microducks are expected to be delivered in time for Christmas, and notes that Nvidia bought Hugging Face in September.
