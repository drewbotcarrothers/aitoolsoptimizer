**Gemini Notebook helps when the exam is about sources you uploaded, and it quizzes you from those sources.** It does not help when you wanted a web search, a tutor that withholds answers across a whole curriculum, or a summary of a lecture you never added. The free plan is enough for a single course if you stay inside the daily caps. Google AI Plus and Pro raise those caps. They do not change the method.

The product was NotebookLM. The limits below are from Google's Gemini Notebook plans table, read October 24, 2026. What changed in the rename is in [NotebookLM is now Gemini Notebook](/blog/notebooklm-is-now-gemini-notebook/). Prices for Google AI Plus ($4.99/month) and Pro ($19.99/month) are from Google's subscriptions page as checked October 16, 2026. We did not run a learning-outcomes study. These workflows follow features Google documents: grounded chat, citations, Audio Overviews, flashcards, quizzes, and reports.

## The free caps that shape a study week

Google's plan table lists these Standard (free Gmail) limits, and says limits can change:

| | Standard (free) | Plus | Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notebooks | 100 per user | 200 | 500 |
| Sources per notebook | 50 | 100 | 300 |
| Chats | 50/day | 200/day | 500/day |
| Audio Overviews | 3/day | 6/day | 20/day |
| Video Overviews | 3/day | 6/day | 20/day, plus 2 cinematic/day |
| Reports, flashcards, quizzes, mind maps | 10/day each | 20/day each | 100/day each |
| Deep Research | 10/month | 3/day | 20/day |

Daily quotas reset after 24 hours. Monthly quotas reset after 30 days. A report, flashcards, an infographic, a slide deck, an audio overview, or a video overview that the product creates automatically when you first add sources is generated once and does not count against the limit.

Three Audio Overviews a day is the constraint that surprises people. Do not spend all three on the first PDF. The seven workflows below assume a free account unless a step says otherwise.

## Set up one notebook per exam

Create the notebook around the test, not around "school." Google documents source-grounded answers with citations. Fifty sources is the free per-notebook cap, which is plenty for a unit and too mixed if you drop in every PDF from the year. Upload the readings, your lecture notes, and the slides. Import Google Docs and Slides from Drive when that is where the professor put them.

Ask a question you already know the answer to, then open the citation. If the click does not land on the sentence you remember, fix the source set before you trust a summary. Audio Overviews can be inaccurate. Google's help page says so directly. The same caution applies to quizzes.

## The seven workflows

### 1. The 10-concept sheet

Prompt: "Using only these sources, list the 10 ideas most likely to be tested. For each, give a two-sentence definition and the source it came from. If two sources disagree, say so."

Then close the notebook and write the ten definitions from memory. The notebook is the answer key, not the study session. This uses chat (50 a day on Standard), which is the abundant resource. Save Audio Overviews for workflow 4.

### 2. A quiz you are supposed to fail

Generate a quiz from the notebook's quiz feature (10 a day on Standard). Take it without the sources open. Anything you missed becomes the next notebook, or a note titled "misses," so the next session is shorter than the whole course.

[Quizlet](/tools/quizlet/) is the better home for a deck you want to flip on your phone for weeks. Gemini Notebook's flashcards (also 10 generations a day on Standard) are tied to this source set. Use them for the week's terms. Move the keepers to a system you will still open after the notebook is stale.

### 3. Teach it in one paragraph

Prompt: "Ask me to explain [concept] as if to a classmate. Then, using only the sources, point out what I skipped. Do not rewrite the explanation for me until I try a second time."

This is the workflow that keeps the tool from doing the assignment. If your course bans AI, skip the product. A school rule beats a useful feature. For drafting an essay, the boundary is the same one in [best AI writing assistants](/blog/best-ai-writing-assistants/): feedback on your sentences, not a submission you did not write.

### 4. One Audio Overview, aimed at the misses

Google's Audio Overview is a spoken discussion of the sources. Formats documented on the help page:

- **Deep Dive**, two hosts, the default
- **Brief**, one speaker, key points in under two minutes
- **Critique**, for an essay or a design doc
- **Debate**, two hosts on opposing sides

You can set a language, choose shorter, default, or longer (longer is English only), and add a prompt so the audio focuses on a topic. Generation runs in the background. You need edit access to create or delete one.

Spend the free plan's 3-a-day cap on the misses from workflow 2, with a prompt such as "Focus on the three mechanisms in chapter 4. Skip the history." Listening is for a walk between classes. It is not a substitute for the pages. Google says the hosts are reflecting the sources, and the audio can still glitch or be wrong. When a line matters, use the citation tools while the audio plays. Google documents that you can look at quotes and query the sources during playback.

### 5. A mind map of one lecture, then a question per branch

Mind maps are 10 a day on Standard. Generate one for a single lecture. Write one question per branch in your own notes. If a branch has no source behind it, delete the branch. The map is a picture of the upload, not of the field.

### 6. Compare two sources that disagree

Prompt: "Source A and Source B both define [term]. Quote the definition from each and list what would change in an answer if I used only one of them."

This is the notebook's actual advantage over a general chatbot. [ChatGPT](/tools/chatgpt/) and [Gemini](/tools/gemini/) will answer from the web and from memory. Gemini Notebook is supposed to stay on the files. Keep the disagreement visible. Courses often test it.

### 7. A one-page report the night before, checked against the syllabus

Reports are 10 a day on Standard. Ask for a one-page brief with headings that match the syllabus topics you paste in, and a final section called "not in the sources." That last section is the list of things you still need from class, not from the model. Deep Research on the free plan is 10 a month. Use it when the notebook is thin and you are allowed to pull more material. Do not use it to replace the assigned readings.

Video Overviews (3 a day on Standard) are optional and easier to over-trust because they look like a finished lesson. Prefer audio or a quiz unless a visual is the thing you are trying to remember.

## When to pay

| You keep hitting | Upgrade | What Google lists |
|---|---|---|
| 3 Audio Overviews | Google AI Plus, $4.99/month | 6/day, 200 chats, 100 sources per notebook |
| A heavy week of quizzes and reports across several classes | Google AI Pro, $19.99/month | 20 Audio Overviews/day, 100 quizzes/day, 300 sources, 500 chats |
| You only wanted web answers | Do not upgrade Notebook | Use [Perplexity](/tools/perplexity/) Free, or Gemini chat |

Ultra's notebook caps are higher still (the help page splits them by the 20 TB and 30 TB Ultra plans, up to 200 Audio Overviews a day on the larger one). That is far past a study habit. The subscription decision for those tiers is in [which AI subscription is worth paying for](/blog/which-ai-subscription-is-worth-paying-for/).

Sharing a notebook does not raise anyone's source limit. Classmates on the free plan still have their own daily caps. Advanced sharing is a paid feature. Custom chat and analytics are available on every plan, including free.

Consumer accounts: Google says your data is not used to train Gemini Notebook unless you send feedback, and feedback can include the surrounding context. Workspace and education accounts have a stricter line: uploads, queries, and responses are not reviewed by people even when you send thumbs-up or thumbs-down, and are not used to train models. Use the account your school issued if the readings are not yours to put in a personal Gmail.

## FAQ

### Is Gemini Notebook good for studying?

It is good when you bring the readings and use it to quiz, compare, and point at citations. It is weak when the notebook is empty and you wanted a general tutor. Pair it with reading, not instead of reading.

### How many Audio Overviews do I get for free?

Google's plan table lists 3 per day on the Standard plan, 6 on Plus, and 20 on Pro. Daily quotas reset after 24 hours. Limits can change. An overview the product auto-generates the first time you add sources does not count.

### Can I use it if my course bans AI?

No. A ban on AI-assisted work covers a grounded notebook the same way it covers a chatbot. Use it only in courses that allow this kind of help, and keep the submitted sentences in your own words.
