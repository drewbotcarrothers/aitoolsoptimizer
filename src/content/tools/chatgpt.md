## What ChatGPT is

ChatGPT is OpenAI's AI assistant. You type, talk or upload files, and it answers, writes, analyzes, codes, creates images and increasingly carries out longer tasks on your behalf. It runs on the web, as desktop apps for Windows and macOS, and on iOS and Android.

The current lineup is built on OpenAI's GPT-6 family. Beyond chat, ChatGPT now bundles **deep research** (multi-step web research reports), **image creation**, **voice** (including voice with video), **projects** and **custom GPTs**, **scheduled tasks**, **Codex** for software work and **ChatGPT Work** for longer agentic tasks on desktop, web and mobile.

## Key features

### Models and reasoning
Free users get unlimited text chats with GPT-6. Plus unlocks advanced reasoning models, and Pro adds "Pro reasoning powered by GPT-6 Astra" with the highest limits.

### Deep research
ChatGPT browses many sources and returns a structured, cited report. Limits rise from Free (limited) to Plus (expanded) to Pro (maximum).

### Images and voice
Create and edit images in chat, and hold spoken conversations, including voice with video on supported devices.

### Projects, tasks and GPTs
Group chats and files into projects, schedule recurring tasks and build or use custom GPTs for specific jobs.

### Codex and ChatGPT Work
Codex works on code in the cloud, CLI, IDE extension and iOS; ChatGPT Work handles longer multi-step jobs. They share a usage allowance.

## Best use cases

- **Writing:** draft a cover letter, then ask for a tighter version in your own voice.
- **Research:** run deep research on "pros and cons of heat pumps in cold climates" and check the cited sources.
- **Learning:** use Study Mode to work through a statistics concept step by step.
- **Coding:** ask Codex to fix a failing test in a GitHub repository.
- **Everyday life:** plan a week of meals around a budget and dietary needs.

## How to get started

1. Sign up free at chatgpt.com or download the app.
2. Set custom instructions so answers match your role and preferred style.
3. Ask a real task, not a test question, and follow up to refine.
4. Upload files (PDFs, spreadsheets) when you need answers grounded in your material.
5. Create a project for ongoing work to keep files and chats together.
6. Upgrade to Go or Plus when you hit limits on the tools you use most.

## Tips and tricks

- **Give context, goal and format:** who it's for, what you need and how long.
- **Ask it to ask you questions** before it writes something important.
- **Verify facts** in sources, especially numbers, quotes and legal or medical topics.
- **Turn on memory carefully** and review what it remembers in settings.

Example prompts:

> I'm a project manager. Turn these meeting notes into a one-page summary with decisions, owners and deadlines in a table. Flag anything ambiguous.

> Explain compound interest to a 14-year-old with one real-life example, then give me three quiz questions to check understanding.
