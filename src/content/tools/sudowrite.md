## What Sudowrite is

Sudowrite is an AI writing partner made by writers for fiction. Instead of a generic chatbot, it offers tools tuned to storytelling problems: flat description, rushed pacing, sentences you've rewritten 100 times, plot holes and blank pages. Its makers describe AI as "a phenomenal collaborator, not a destroyer of worlds."

The centerpiece is **Story Bible**, which guides you from an idea to an outline, to chapter beats, to thousands of words written in your style.

## Key features

### Story Bible
Plan and draft long-form fiction step by step, keeping characters, worldbuilding and outline in one place.

### Write
"Autocomplete on steroids": suggests the next 300 words in your voice, with options.

### Describe and Expand
Add sensory description without bogging down the story, and build out rushed scenes for better pacing.

### Rewrite
A flexible revision buddy that takes direction.

### Feedback
Reads your text in about 10 seconds and gives five actionable areas to improve.

### Brainstorm, Canvas and Visualize
Generate ideas, explore plot twists and character secrets, and create art from character sheets and worldbuilding docs.

## Best use cases

- **Novelists:** draft chapters from a Story Bible outline.
- **Short-story writers:** expand a scene or try alternate endings.
- **Fan-fiction and genre writers:** brainstorm twists and characters.
- **Revisers:** get quick, structured feedback on drafts.

## How to get started

1. Sign up at sudowrite.com and start the free trial.
2. Paste a chapter or start a new Story Bible.
3. Fill in characters, setting and synopsis.
4. Use Write or Expand on a scene and pick the best option.
5. Run Feedback and revise in your own voice.

## Tips and tricks

- **Feed Story Bible detail**: the more context, the better the voice.
- **Use Describe sparingly** for key moments.
- **Treat suggestions as drafts**, not final text.
- **Track credits** on long generation runs.

Example Brainstorm prompt:

> Give me 10 secrets the lighthouse keeper could be hiding that would explain why ships keep disappearing, ranging from mundane to supernatural.
